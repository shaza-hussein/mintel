MinTel System Blueprint

Autonomous Telecom Product Intelligence Platform

A complete business and technical solution for generating new telecom bundles, selecting the right customers for each bundle, and simulating financial and market outcomes before launch.

Non-negotiable design rule: all prices are produced by the existing Dynamic Pricing Algorithm. MinTel does not override price. It uses your pricing output as the commercial anchor, then optimizes bundle design, targeting, and launch strategy around it.
Primary Business Goal Maximize incremental revenue and contribution margin
Core Decision Layers Bundle generation, offer targeting, simulation and governance
Main Users Marketing, product, pricing, analytics, campaign and strategy teams
Operating Style Human-supervised autonomy with closed-loop learning

01

Executive Summary

MinTel should be built as a decision platform, not as a single model. The system needs three tightly connected engines: Bundle Generation, Customer-Offer Recommendation, and a Simulation Engine. These engines must share one product schema, one price source, one customer state model, and one KPI framework.

What the System Solves

Telecom product design is usually slow, manual, and fragmented. Marketing identifies demand, analysts study data, product teams design bundles, pricing teams set prices, and campaign teams guess which customers should receive them. MinTel replaces that fragmented workflow with one integrated decision process.

What the System Produces

For every launch cycle, MinTel produces:

  • new bundle candidates designed for real customer needs
  • recommended audience lists for each bundle
  • expected adoption, revenue, churn impact, and cannibalization
  • pre-launch simulations across best, base, and worst market scenarios
  • clear approval-ready recommendations for marketing teams
Recommended business positioning: MinTel is an Autonomous Telecom Product Intelligence Platform that designs offers, matches them to the right customers, and predicts business outcomes before launch.
02

Strategic Challenges

The system should directly address two telecom pain points: product innovation speed and customer targeting precision.

Challenge A: Product and Bundle Creation

  • Manual product creation is too slow for a highly competitive telecom market.
  • Bundle design is often based on historical intuition, not a dynamic optimization process.
  • Pricing, design, and launch are separated across teams, which creates delay.
  • Operators lose revenue when they cannot react quickly to demand shifts or competitor moves.
  • Many bundles are launched without a strong forecast of adoption, cannibalization, or margin quality.

Challenge B: Customer Targeting and Personalization

  • Broad segmentation treats many different customers as if they behave the same way.
  • Campaigns often target likely buyers instead of customers whose behavior can be changed.
  • Offer relevance is weak when there is no customer-level next-best-offer policy.
  • Generic campaigns reduce ROI, hurt engagement, and waste channel budget.
  • Without simulation, teams cannot see the effect of targeting before launch.

Revenue Risk

Missed upsell, cross-sell, retention, and win-back opportunities due to delayed or weak offer decisions.

Portfolio Risk

Poorly differentiated bundles can cannibalize existing plans and increase product complexity.

Execution Risk

Launching without scenario simulation exposes the operator to commercial, customer, and network risk.

06

Bundle Generation Engine

This is the part that creates new telecom bundles. It must be creative enough to discover revenue opportunities, but constrained enough to remain commercially valid and operationally launchable.

How Bundle Generation Should Work

  1. Start with a business objective such as acquisition, upsell, retention, win-back, youth growth, roaming monetization, or enterprise penetration.
  2. Identify opportunity patterns from usage, churn, demand gaps, campaign outcomes, and portfolio weaknesses.
  3. Generate bundle candidates inside a fixed telecom bundle grammar.
  4. Send each candidate to the Dynamic Pricing Algorithm for the official price.
  5. Estimate adoption, margin, retention impact, and cannibalization.
  6. Choose a final portfolio of bundles rather than approving one bundle independently.

Bundle Grammar: What a Bundle Can Contain

  • mobile data allowance, voice allowance, SMS allowance
  • validity period: daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, seasonal
  • prepaid, postpaid, hybrid, family, youth, SME, enterprise, roaming, tourist
  • digital add-ons: streaming, gaming, cloud, education, social, productivity
  • fair usage policy, speed tier, hotspot, rollover, off-peak bonuses
  • device linkage, loyalty rewards, renewal incentives, recharge coupling
  • channel restrictions, region restrictions, customer eligibility rules

Hard Constraints

  • cannot break regulatory rules
  • cannot violate network capacity limits
  • cannot create impossible fulfillment combinations
  • cannot launch below margin floor unless explicitly approved

Soft Constraints

  • keep bundle portfolio understandable for customers
  • limit internal complexity for campaign and sales teams
  • preserve brand position and product hierarchy
  • avoid over-fragmenting the catalog

Scoring Dimensions

  • expected revenue uplift
  • expected contribution margin
  • retention impact
  • adoption confidence
  • cannibalization risk
  • strategic value
Important commercial rule: the system should optimize a bundle portfolio, not just a single bundle. A bundle can look strong alone but still damage total revenue if it cannibalizes the rest of the catalog.
07

Customer Targeting and Recommendation Engine

After the best bundles are selected, the next decision is: which bundle should be recommended to which customer, through which channel, and at what time?

Targeting Objective

Do not optimize for customers who are merely likely to buy. Optimize for customers whose behavior can be improved by the offer. The system should estimate incremental business impact at the customer level and recommend the next-best-offer accordingly.

  • next-best-offer selection
  • next-best-channel selection
  • next-best-time selection
  • contact policy optimization
  • budget-aware campaign allocation

Customer Features the Engine Should Use

  • current product and tenure
  • usage profile: voice-heavy, data-heavy, mixed, low-engagement
  • recharge and billing behavior
  • device type and capability
  • churn risk and loyalty signals
  • channel response history
  • price sensitivity and affordability patterns
  • household, SME, or shared-account context

Decision 1

Eligibility filter
Remove customers who cannot legally or operationally receive the offer.

Decision 2

Offer uplift estimate
Estimate how much each bundle changes expected revenue or retention for that customer.

Decision 3

Policy ranking
Choose the best bundle after considering contact fatigue, channel cost, and campaign limits.

Decision 4

Execution package
Send the final audience list with reason codes and confidence to campaign tools.

Do not use only classic segmentation. Segments are useful for reporting and strategy, but campaign decisions should be made at customer level whenever possible.
Targeting Output Why It Matters
Customer ID + Recommended Bundle Defines the next-best-offer per customer rather than using one bundle for everyone.
Expected Incremental Revenue Keeps campaign optimization tied to business value.
Expected Retention Effect Prevents short-term revenue decisions from increasing future churn.
Preferred Channel Improves acceptance and lowers communication cost.
Reason Code Makes the recommendation interpretable for marketing teams.
Confidence or Risk Band Supports approval and prioritization decisions.

Simulation Engine and Market Digital Twin

The simulation engine is what makes MinTel strategically powerful. It lets the marketing team test a new telecom bundle before launching it in the real market.

What the Simulation Must Include

  • customer agents with heterogeneous behavior
  • current portfolio and newly proposed bundles
  • channel strategy and campaign budget
  • competitor reactions and market pressure
  • network load and quality implications
  • renewal, upgrade, downgrade, and churn behavior over time

Simulation Outputs for Decision Makers

  • expected adoption by week and month
  • incremental revenue and margin over 30, 90, and 180 days
  • retention impact and churn delta
  • cannibalization against existing bundles
  • network stress or capacity pressure
  • best case, base case, and worst case scenario bands
1

Define Scenario

Select bundles, target groups, channels, budgets, timing, and market assumptions.

2

Initialize Market State

Load customer states, product portfolio, current network conditions, and competitor baseline.

3

Expose Offers

Simulate which customers actually see the offer through the chosen channels.

4

Model Decisions

Customers accept, ignore, postpone, switch, or churn based on their individual state.

5

Update Revenue

Track immediate and future revenue, costs, and margin from bundle adoption.

6

Update Network Load

Measure whether the new bundle changes traffic, congestion, or quality in key areas.

7

Apply Competitor Events

Test competitor discounting, copying, stronger retention action, or channel pressure.

8

Compare Outcomes

Present scenario results to marketing with risk bands and recommended action.

Scenario Type 1

Commercial Scenario
What if we launch bundle A and B together versus bundle A alone?

Scenario Type 2

Competitive Scenario
What if a competitor drops price or introduces a similar youth package?

Scenario Type 3

Operational Scenario
What if network capacity is tight in high-usage cells after launch?

Simulation principle: show probability ranges, not one point estimate. Marketing teams need uncertainty-aware decisions, not fake certainty.

Data Domains and Contracts

Since you already have access to telecom databases, the key challenge is not access. It is making the data usable, consistent, timely, and governed for decision automation.

Critical Data Domains

  • customer master data and account hierarchy
  • subscription and product history
  • usage detail aggregated to customer-level features
  • recharge, billing, payment, debt, and revenue history
  • campaign exposure, response, and conversion events
  • network quality, congestion, and location indicators
  • customer service interactions and complaint signals
  • competitor observations and market events

Data Contract Rules

  • standardized customer identifier across systems
  • bundle schema versioning for every candidate and launched offer
  • event timestamps with reliable time ordering
  • clear definitions for revenue, margin, churn, and conversion
  • audit trail linking bundle generation, pricing, targeting, and campaign outcomes
  • feature freshness standards for real-time, daily, and monthly decisions

KPI Framework

MinTel should be measured as a business system, not just as an AI system. Success must be visible in commercial, customer, and operational metrics.

Revenue KPIs

Incremental Revenue Revenue generated because of the new bundle versus control or baseline.
Incremental Contribution Margin Revenue after direct cost and offer economics.
Portfolio Revenue Uplift Total uplift after considering cannibalization and migration effects.

Customer KPIs

Adoption Rate Share of targeted customers who activate the recommended bundle.
Retention Lift Reduction in churn or downgrade probability after bundle adoption.
Offer Relevance Score Proxy for how well the recommendation matches real customer need.

Marketing KPIs

Campaign ROI Incremental margin relative to campaign and channel cost.
Cost per Incremental Conversion True campaign efficiency measure.
Channel Efficiency Performance by SMS, app, web, retail, call center, and other channels.

Portfolio KPIs

Cannibalization Rate Share of adopters who would have generated similar value on an existing bundle.
Catalog Simplicity Index Measures whether the product set remains understandable and manageable.
Launch Quality Score Composite metric for margin, risk, adoption confidence, and strategic fit.

Operational KPIs

Time to Design New Bundle Reduction in product creation cycle time.
Time to Target Campaign Speed from bundle approval to audience selection.
Forecast Accuracy Difference between simulated and actual launch outcomes.

Governance KPIs

Decision Explainability Rate Share of recommendations with valid reason codes and traceability.
Override Rate How often humans reject system recommendations and why.
Model Stability and Drift Whether performance stays reliable across time and market shifts.

Governance, Risk, and Controls

A powerful autonomous system needs control points. Governance is not a side feature; it is part of the architecture.

Human Approval Gates

  • new bundle approval before catalog publication
  • exception approval for low-margin or high-risk launches
  • campaign approval before large-scale targeting
  • simulation review before major product change
  • rollback authority if early launch signals are weak

Guardrails

  • margin floor thresholds
  • maximum allowed cannibalization
  • network utilization limits
  • contact fatigue and channel frequency caps
  • restricted offers for sensitive or regulated customer groups

Auditability

Every launch decision should be traceable from generated bundle to price, audience, scenario results, approval, and final outcome.

Explainability

Marketing users should see why a bundle was proposed, why a customer was targeted, and why a scenario is strong or weak.

Rollback

The system must support safe shutdown, bundle withdrawal, campaign stop, and policy revert if live metrics deteriorate.

Main risk to avoid: three disconnected models producing inconsistent answers. Bundle generation, targeting, and simulation must use the same definitions for bundle, customer state, and business KPIs.

Operating Model and Team Structure

MinTel will only work if business and technical ownership are both clear. This is not purely a data science project.

Marketing Lead Defines objectives, approves campaigns, interprets simulations.
Product Manager Owns bundle strategy, catalog fit, and launch prioritization.
Pricing Owner Owns the Dynamic Pricing Algorithm and pricing governance.
Data Science Lead Owns targeting, scoring, simulation logic, and measurement.
Data Engineer / MLOps Owns pipelines, feature store, deployment, monitoring, and retraining.
Commercial Governance Owns policy, risk thresholds, audit, and approval controls.

How Teams Should Work

  • Marketing defines intent and reviews launch alternatives.
  • Product teams define bundle policies, templates, and constraints.
  • Pricing remains the authority for price determination.
  • Data science builds decision logic and simulation.
  • Commercial governance approves exceptions and monitors risk.

Best Decision Forum

Create a weekly or bi-weekly Offer Intelligence Council where MinTel recommendations are reviewed, approved, launched, or rejected with a documented reason. This keeps the system aligned with real business priorities.

Implementation Roadmap

Build MinTel in controlled phases. Do not try to automate everything on day one.

Phase 1

Foundation

Define the bundle schema, customer feature store, KPI dictionary, and pricing integration contract. Build the first version of the product opportunity and bundle generation workflow.

Phase 2

Bundle Intelligence

Add commercial scoring, catalog constraints, portfolio optimization, and bundle approval workflow. Start generating new bundles with price anchored by the pricing algorithm.

Phase 3

Targeting Intelligence

Build customer-level offer recommendation, channel selection, control groups, and campaign measurement loops. Move from segment-based targeting to next-best-offer policy.

Phase 4

Simulation Digital Twin

Build scenario planning, competitor reactions, network stress forecasting, and uncertainty-aware launch reports. Validate the simulator with historical launches.

Phase 5

Controlled Autonomy

Allow the system to auto-recommend or partially auto-execute low-risk decisions inside strict guardrails, with human approval for high-impact launches.

Recommended first milestone: deliver one fully working use case end-to-end, such as monthly prepaid upsell bundles or retention bundles for high-data churn-risk users. One closed-loop success is better than five incomplete modules.

Audience View

Scenario View

Final Positioning Statement

MinTel should be presented as an intelligent telecom decision platform that connects bundle innovation, personalized targeting, and market simulation into one operating system for commercial growth.

The strongest version of MinTel is not "an AI that creates telecom offers." It is a controlled autonomous system that: finds opportunities, creates bundles, uses your pricing engine for price, chooses the right customers, simulates the launch, and learns from outcomes.

If these parts share one common logic and one common KPI layer, MinTel becomes a real product strategy platform. If they are built separately, it becomes three isolated demos.
